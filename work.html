UC San Francisco · Higher Education

A university-wide Drupal platform, grown from zero

John's flagship engagement — 20+ years at UCSF, ending as Website Services Manager

When UCSF needed a way to serve hundreds of departments without hundreds of bespoke sites, John built the answer: a Drupal multisite platform architected to be the university's standard web service — designed, automated, and supported as a product rather than a one-off build.

The platform grew to serve the breadth of campus, moving launches and upgrades that once took weeks to minutes through automated deployment. Today the platform is run by UCSF's own staff, with Acquia handling hosting — a service built to outlast its architect.

Higher Ed & Public Sector · Eric's Track Record

Complex digital operations for research universities and agencies

24+ years of platform, UX, and governance work

Eric has spent 24+ years building sites and tools for complex higher ed, academic research, and public sector environments — 19+ of them with content management systems, and over a decade leading web and digital communications operations and cross-functional teams.

His work spans information architecture, content strategy, accessibility, DevOps, and workflow design — the full connective tissue between a platform and the people who publish on it every day.

Today he is the Product Owner of UCSF's Platform for Academic Websites (PAWS) — the university's initiative to move its 30+ academic units off a "fend for yourself" landscape of aging, end-of-life sites and onto one scalable Drupal platform: centralized hosting, a shared codebase, UCSF-wide integrations, and consistent branding, with compliance built into the platform rather than audited into it after the fact. It's the platform-as-a-product playbook — the same approach John used to build UCSF's first site platform, now aimed at the academic side of the house.

California Health Care Foundation (CHCF) · Healthcare Philanthropy

Website strategy and continuous improvement, as an ongoing partner

Current engagement — this is what "supported for life" looks like

CHCF's work depends on trust and accessibility — health policy research aimed at communities who need information to be clear, current, and easy to reach. John works with the foundation's communications team, led by Louise Chu, to develop website strategy and project-manages the ongoing program of improvements to the site.

No dramatic redesign, no big reveal — a markedly improved pace of delivered improvements at a lower cost than the previous vendor. Recent work includes migrating the site's WAF from Fastly to Cloudflare, cutting average daily unwanted bot traffic to the site by roughly 70%. It's the engagement model we offer every client: a long-term partner who knows your context, not a vendor you brief from scratch each time.

Where it all started — 1996

John managed the Sierra Club's website and online donations in the earliest days of the web — when "digital strategy" meant explaining to people what a website was for, and online fundraising meant building trust in a channel nobody had used for giving before.
